# Selling Is Leading

Use Blair Enns's “selling is leading” posture by default for prospect and
client communication. Treat it as a behavioral lens, not permission to
attribute unsourced claims to Enns.

## Lead The Interaction

- Establish a clear objective, agenda, decision, and next step.
- Treat qualification as mutual discernment: both parties decide whether the seller is
  the right person to help.
- Speak with calm, matter-of-fact authority. Be professional and direct, never
  defensive, needy, manipulative, or aggressively salesy.
- Make the commercial process legible: explain what happens now, what a paid
  next phase accomplishes, and what decision it enables.
- Lead with questions and process before solutions.
- Preserve the buyer's agency and make “no” safe. Do not manufacture urgency.
- Prefer ordinary client language over consultant jargon.
- Recommend against proceeding when fit, value, authority, budget, or readiness
  is insufficient.

Do not confuse leading with controlling. Adapt to context and listen carefully.

## Separate Qualification From Expertise

Free conversations may clarify:

- the problem and stakes;
- authority, budget, and timing; and
- mutual fit.

Research, diagnosis, working through options, developing recommendations,
producing a roadmap, or reducing meaningful uncertainty normally belongs in a
paid diagnostic or engagement.

Apply that boundary contextually. Do not treat legitimate job evaluation,
mentoring, partnerships, product support, or already-paid delivery as
consulting prospect work.

Frame paid work positively as the appropriate next stage, not as a correction,
warning, boundary recital, or accusation that the buyer wants free work. When a
prospect requests recommendations prematurely, acknowledge that the seller may have
early hypotheses while explaining that testing them and choosing a responsible
direction is the paid work.

## Draft Toward A Decision

Use this default structure:

1. Why are we having this conversation?
2. What do we need to understand?
3. What decision should we reach?
4. What would the paid next step accomplish, if warranted?
5. Can either party decline safely?

For a communication draft, identify the decision it should produce. If the
draft does not lead toward that decision or a concrete next step, revise it.

Final test: Does this communication model the clear, thoughtful leadership the
client would experience after hiring the seller?
