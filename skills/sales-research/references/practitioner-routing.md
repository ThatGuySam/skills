# Practitioner Routing

## Registry Contract

Search the active project's configured sources for a document titled
`Sales Practitioner Registry`. For each selected practitioner, read:

- tags;
- best use; and
- limitation.

The registry outranks this shortlist for practitioner metadata when it is
present and current. If it is missing, use these routes to select candidates
and retrieve their primary work. Do not invent missing registry fields or
present this routing reference as evidence that a practitioner made a specific
claim.

## Situation Routes

- **Premature ideas or recommendations:** Start with Blair Enns, Peter Block,
  and Brent Ozar. Add Karl Sakas, Brennan Dunn, Paul Swail, or Jason Liu only to
  shape the appropriate paid next phase.
- **AI feasibility, RAG, agents, evals, or POCs:** Start with Jason Liu, Peter
  Cohan, and Hamel Husain or Shreya Shankar. Add Jugurtha Hadjar or Doug
  Turnbull for field reality.
- **Broad or unreferable positioning:** Start with David C. Baker and Philip
  Morgan. Add April Dunford for competitive language or Erik Dietrich for the
  code-labor trap.
- **Price anxiety or self-created concessions:** Start with Jonathan Stark,
  Alan Weiss, and David A. Fields. Require a value, scope, risk, or commitment
  trade before recommending a price change.
- **Discovery and qualification:** Start with Mahan Khalsa and Randy Illig. Add
  Neil Rackham for complex sales, Bob Moesta for the buyer's struggling moment,
  or Rob Fitzpatrick for founder and customer evidence.
- **Enterprise or government:** Start with Khalsa and Illig plus Rackham. Add
  John McMahon for authority and procurement, Hadjar for ML readiness, or
  Matthew Dixon and Ted McKenna only after genuine preference exists.
- **Paid discovery or a first engagement:** Start with Peter Block, Brennan
  Dunn, and Karl Sakas. Add Paul Swail, Tom Critchlow, or Paul Boag for
  technical, workshop, or buyer-language examples.
- **Demo or proof request:** Use Peter Cohan to choose the minimum adequate
  proof, Hadjar to structure a paid tranche, and Husain or Shankar to ground
  technical credibility.
- **SaaS or product:** Start with April Dunford, Pete Kazanjy, Bob Moesta, and
  Patrick McKenzie. Do not default to bespoke-consulting diagnostics.
- **AI-era pricing or business model:** Start with Jonathan Stark and Tim
  Williams. Add Enns for the sale and Liu for AI-consulting reality.
- **Trust or listening:** Pair Charles H. Green or Michael Bungay Stanier with
  Enns, Block, Ozar, or Khalsa so helpfulness still leads to a decision.
- **Employment or recruiting:** Use relevant positioning and negotiation
  sources plus the user's current constraints. Legitimate technical evaluation is
  not automatically a request for free consulting.

## Expand The Bench

Prefer:

1. primary work from named practitioners who sold comparable software, AI, or
   professional-services work;
2. detailed first-person cases with buyer context, project shape, dates,
   numbers, failures, and tradeoffs;
3. practitioner interviews, transcripts, Hacker News or Lobsters discussions,
   and focused community threads; and
4. primary technical, procurement, legal, or research sources when delivery
   reality changes the sale.

Reject generic SEO pages, AI roundups, unsupported viral advice, manipulative
pressure tactics, and material that mainly sells the reviewed method. Label
self-reported results and inference. Let a new source outrank the bench only
when closer domain fit and stronger evidence justify it.
