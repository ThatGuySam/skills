# Clear writing and STE requests

Use this reference for ASD-STE100, controlled-language, or technical clarity rewrites.

## Default: STE-inspired

Use short sentences, concrete verbs, consistent terminology, and explicit subjects. Prefer one instruction per step. Put conditions before the affected action when this prevents an unsafe or ambiguous reading. Keep adjacent sentences connected; splitting every clause can make an explanation harder to follow.

Preserve technical names and distinctions. Expand long noun clusters when that clarifies the relationship. Replace vague pronouns when their referent is uncertain. Keep the original numbers, units, sequence, exceptions, warnings, and degree of certainty. Do not quietly repair contradictory source instructions; identify the conflict.

Before returning a rewrite, compare each actionable or factual statement with the source. Check especially `not`, `only`, `unless`, `until`, maximum values, and simultaneous actions.

Example, explicitly illustrative:

Original: “Do not restart unless the pressure is below 2 bar and the indicator is green.”

Rewrite: “Restart only when both conditions are true: the pressure is below 2 bar, and the indicator is green.”

The rewrite retains both conditions and the strict boundary. “2 bar or lower” would change the instruction.

## Strict ASD-STE100

[ASD-STE100](https://www.asd-ste100.org/) is a specific controlled language with writing rules and a dictionary. General plain language, an infographic, or a model's familiarity with the name is not a compliance check.

If strict compliance matters, establish the applicable issue and obtain the complete official specification, including its dictionary and rules for technical names and verbs. Check vocabulary by meaning and part of speech as well as grammar and sentence construction. Report what was actually checked. If the specification is inaccessible, deliver a clearly labeled STE-inspired draft and identify strict verification as incomplete.

Issue 9 is dated 15 January 2025 in the official material researched for this skill. Check the current issue when compliance is requested. Do not redistribute the dictionary as part of this skill.

The reference infographic in the originating research marks TEST as an approved verb. The official Issue 9 dictionary instead lists TEST as a noun and directs the unapproved verb toward alternatives. Do not copy that chart as authority. Nor does this correction establish that the rest of the chart is correct.

“80% STE” means a relaxed style request, not a measurable compliance score. Explain it as STE-inspired writing when the distinction matters.
