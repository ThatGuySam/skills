These are original, short summaries of current Emily Post Institute pages plus separately labeled skill synthesis, checked on 2026-08-09. They are not a copied modern corpus, not quotations, and not Emily Post's own words. Attribute only a `Current-tradition summary` to the Institute. ## Core principles

**Current-tradition summary:** The Institute presents etiquette as manners that change with circumstances, tested against consideration, respect, and honesty.

**Skill synthesis:** Use those principles to test whether an old form still serves the people affected now.

Sources:

- https://emilypost.com/advice/the-principles-of-etiquette
- https://emilypost.com/about/definition-of-etiquette-consideration-respect-and-honesty

## Names, titles, and pronouns

**Current-tradition summary:** Use the names and pronouns people ask others to use. Ask respectfully when uncertain; after a mistake, correct it briefly and continue. The Institute describes including one's own pronouns in an email signature as useful.

**Skill synthesis:** Personal disclosure remains voluntary. Whether or not someone includes pronouns in a signature, use the names and pronouns they request.

Source: https://emilypost.com/advice/guide-to-using-pronouns-and-properly-addressing-our-gender-diverse-world

## Boundaries and refusal

**Current-tradition summary:** A refusal can be expressed graciously, truthfully, and clearly.

**Skill synthesis:** Graciousness does not require compliance. A candid no may be more considerate than delay, resentment, a fabricated emergency, or a promise that will not be kept. Keep the explanation proportionate to the relationship and obligation.

Source: https://emilypost.com/advice/how-to-say-no-graciously

## Cross-cultural situations

**Current-tradition summary:** Cross-cultural etiquette requires attention to differing cultural expectations rather than assuming one set of manners is universal.

**Skill synthesis:** Principles can travel more readily than manners. Research the local setting, follow an informed host when appropriate, and ask rather than assume when customs differ.

Source: https://emilypost.com/training-and-services/cross-cultural-etiquette

## Digital manners

**Current-tradition summary:** The Institute treats digital manners as applying etiquette principles to technology-mediated relationships.

**Skill synthesis:** Consider attention, urgency, persistence, privacy, audience, interruption, accessibility, and whether a richer or more private channel would reduce harm.

Source: https://emilypost.com/advice/digital-manners

## Modern analogy map — skill synthesis

| Historical form | Possible modern analogue | What must be reconsidered |
|---|---|---|
| Calling card or formal visit | Follow-up text, email, direct message, connection request | privacy, interruption, platform context, accessibility |
| Written invitation and reply | Event app, calendar invite, group text, email RSVP | whether it is actually an invitation, deadlines, guest count, plus-ones |
| Letter of introduction | Email introduction, referral, LinkedIn message | consent before sharing contact details, power, implied endorsement |
| House party or country-house stay | Staying with friends, shared rental, group trip | duration, money, chores, sleeping space, opt-out, invisible labor |
| Chaperon | Safety plan, trusted companion, event safeguarding | adult autonomy; do not revive gender control |
| Rank- or gender-based precedence | Host responsibility and explicit accommodation | role, need, access, age only when relevant—not inherited status |
| Servant/household rules | Employment and service interactions | labor rights, dignity, compensation, boundaries, no surveillance norm |
| Formal mourning rules | Support after a death | bereaved person's wishes, culture/religion, practical help, privacy |

## Paid household work

**Skill synthesis:** Historical courtesy is never enough. Fair pay, a realistic workload, rest, safety, privacy, a right to decline added or hazardous work, and applicable current labor law, contract, and agency terms override the historical arrangement. State the legal priority without inventing jurisdiction-specific legal advice.
