This practice capture points to memory swapping as the likely cause of the browser freezes. CPU capacity is plentiful: 87–88% is idle.

Safari uses about 19 GB of the Mac’s 32 GB RAM. Swap holds about 9 GB, and each five-second interval moves roughly 0.9 GiB into RAM and another 0.9 GiB out. That sustained movement can stall large tabs when you switch between them. Swap usage alone would be weaker evidence; the ongoing traffic matters here.

The most useful next step is to close a few large Safari tabs or windows, then try switching tabs again. Improvement would support memory pressure as the cause. Disk latency, temperature, and network activity were not captured, so their contribution remains unknown.

These are synthetic practice measurements, not a diagnosis of your live Mac.
